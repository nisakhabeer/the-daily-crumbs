25+ Christmas Dessert Table Ideas for a Stunning Display

Oct 24, 2026

A Christmas dessert table is the room everyone drifts back to — one beautiful cake in the middle, tiers of little things to graze on, and something warm to sip while the music plays. Part dessert spread and part decoration, it sets the mood for the whole party before a single slice is cut.

These are the desserts that make the display work, chosen to look stunning together and to be made ahead so the table is set before the doorbell rings. There are showstoppers to anchor the middle, plenty of grab-and-go minis for grazing, and a warm station at the end — everything you need to build a Christmas dessert table guests will circle all night long.

1. Red Velvet Showstopper Cake

Deep red layers, snowy cream cheese frosting and a crown of berries — this is the cake that anchors a Christmas dessert table like nothing else. Set it in the center on your tallest stand and let everything else arrange itself around it.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 1/2 cups sugar
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda and 1 teaspoon cocoa powder
  • 1 cup buttermilk and 1 1/2 cups neutral oil
  • 2 eggs and 2 tablespoons red food coloring
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract and 1 teaspoon white vinegar
  • 16 oz cream cheese and 1/2 cup butter, softened
  • 4 cups powdered sugar
  • White chocolate curls or sugared cranberries, to crown

Directions

  1. Whisk the dry ingredients, then beat in the buttermilk, oil, eggs, coloring, vanilla and vinegar. Divide between three lined 8-inch pans.
  2. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 22 to 25 minutes, until a tester comes out clean. Cool completely.
  3. Beat the cream cheese, butter, powdered sugar and a splash of vanilla into a smooth frosting.
  4. Layer the cakes with frosting, add a thin crumb coat and chill 30 minutes, then frost smooth and crown with white chocolate curls or sugared cranberries.

2. Classic Yule Log

A chocolate sponge rolled around whipped chocolate cream and dressed up as a snowy log, bark lines and all. It slices into pretty spirals and brings instant woodland charm to the display.

Ingredients

  • 6 eggs, separated
  • 3/4 cup sugar, divided
  • 1/2 cup cocoa powder and 1/4 cup flour
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 1/2 cups heavy cream
  • 8 oz dark chocolate, melted, for the filling and ganache
  • 1/2 cup butter, softened
  • Powdered sugar, for snow
  • Meringue mushrooms or sugared berries, to decorate

Directions

  1. Beat the yolks with half the sugar, fold in cocoa and flour, then fold in whites beaten with the remaining sugar. Spread in a lined jelly-roll pan and bake at 375°F (190°C) for 10 to 12 minutes.
  2. Turn the warm sponge onto a cocoa-dusted towel and roll it up while warm. Cool rolled.
  3. Whip the cream, fold in half the melted chocolate, unroll the sponge, fill and roll again.
  4. Cover with ganache made from the remaining chocolate and butter, drag a fork through it for bark lines, dust with powdered sugar snow and add mushrooms or berries.

3. Christmas Trifle in a Glass Bowl

All those layers — sponge, custard, berries and cream — showing through the glass are the decoration, no piping skills required. Build it in the morning and let the flavors settle while you set the table.

Ingredients

  • 1 sponge or pound cake, cut into cubes
  • 2 cups prepared custard or vanilla pudding
  • 3 cups mixed berries, plus extra for the top
  • 1 small packet strawberry jelly, made and set (optional)
  • 1/4 cup orange juice or sherry, for sprinkling
  • 2 cups heavy cream, whipped with 2 tablespoons sugar
  • Chocolate shavings, to finish

Directions

  1. Line the bottom of a glass trifle bowl with sponge cubes and sprinkle with orange juice or sherry.
  2. Add a layer of jelly if using, then berries, then custard. Repeat the layers, keeping them neat against the glass.
  3. Chill at least 4 hours so the layers set and the sponge softens.
  4. Top with whipped cream, a pile of fresh berries and chocolate shavings just before serving.

4. Mini Cheesecake Bites, Three Toppings

Two-bite cheesecakes in muffin cups, finished three ways so the tray looks like a tiny tasting menu. Cranberry for sparkle, chocolate for the classicists and caramel for the sweet tooths.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups graham cracker crumbs and 5 tablespoons melted butter
  • 16 oz cream cheese, softened
  • 1/2 cup sugar and 1/4 cup sour cream
  • 2 eggs and 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup cranberry sauce, for topping
  • 1/2 cup melted chocolate or ganache, for topping
  • 1/2 cup caramel sauce and flaky salt, for topping

Directions

  1. Press the crumb mixture into lined muffin cups and bake at 325°F (165°C) for 5 minutes.
  2. Beat the filling smooth, divide over the crusts and bake 15 to 18 minutes, until just set with a slight wobble.
  3. Cool, then chill at least 3 hours or overnight.
  4. Top a third with cranberry sauce, a third with chocolate and a third with caramel and a pinch of salt. Arrange in rows on a platter.

5. Pavlova Wreath

A crisp meringue ring filled with clouds of cream and a tumble of red berries — the wreath you can eat. It is the lightest thing on the table and usually the first to vanish.

Ingredients

  • 6 egg whites, at room temperature
  • 1 1/2 cups superfine sugar
  • 2 teaspoons cornstarch and 1 teaspoon white vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 1/2 cups heavy cream, whipped
  • 2 cups red berries — strawberries, raspberries and cherries
  • Fresh mint leaves and pomegranate seeds, to finish

Directions

  1. Beat the whites to soft peaks, add the sugar a spoonful at a time and beat to stiff, glossy peaks. Fold in cornstarch, vinegar and vanilla.
  2. Pipe or spoon the meringue in a ring on a parchment-lined tray, making a shallow channel in the middle.
  3. Bake at 250°F (120°C) for 1 hour 15 minutes, then turn the oven off and let it cool inside completely.
  4. Fill the wreath with whipped cream and pile on the berries, mint and pomegranate seeds just before serving.

6. Peppermint Bark Slabs

Dark and white chocolate layered thin, showered with crushed candy canes and broken into dramatic shards. Stand the big pieces upright at the back of a tray and it looks like a candy shop window.

Ingredients

  • 12 oz dark chocolate, chopped
  • 12 oz white chocolate, chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon peppermint extract
  • 6 candy canes, crushed
  • Pinch of flaky salt (optional)

Directions

  1. Melt the dark chocolate, stir in the peppermint extract and spread thin on a parchment-lined baking sheet. Chill 10 minutes, until just set.
  2. Melt the white chocolate and spread over the dark layer, swirling the edges together lightly.
  3. Scatter the crushed candy canes over the top while still wet and chill until firm, about 30 minutes.
  4. Break into large display shards and stack them on a tray or stand.

7. Chocolate Truffle Tower

Silky ganache truffles rolled in cocoa and crushed candy, stacked into a little pyramid. They are rich, so one or two with coffee is exactly right — and the tower makes them look like a patisserie display.

Ingredients

  • 16 oz dark chocolate, finely chopped
  • 1 cup heavy cream
  • 2 tablespoons butter, softened
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract or a splash of liqueur
  • Cocoa powder, for coating
  • Crushed candy canes and chopped nuts, for coating

Directions

  1. Pour hot cream over the chocolate, rest 2 minutes, then stir into a smooth ganache. Stir in butter and vanilla.
  2. Chill until firm enough to scoop, about 2 hours.
  3. Roll teaspoonfuls into balls and toss in cocoa, crushed candy or nuts.
  4. Stack the truffles in a pyramid on a small stand or in a wide bowl, and keep chilled until serving.

8. Edible Gingerbread House Centerpiece

One handsome gingerbread house — or a little snowy row of them — right in the middle of the table, and the decorating is done. Build it a few days ahead; it keeps beautifully and perfumes the whole spread with spice.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon ground ginger and 1 tablespoon cinnamon
  • 3/4 cup molasses and 3/4 cup brown sugar
  • 1/2 cup butter and 1 egg
  • 2 cups powdered sugar and 1 egg white, for royal icing
  • Assorted candies, sprinkles and candy canes, to decorate
  • Extra powdered sugar, for snow

Directions

  1. Melt the butter, molasses and sugar, cool slightly, then mix with the flour, spices and egg into a stiff dough. Chill 1 hour.
  2. Roll out, cut your wall and roof panels, and bake at 350°F (175°C) for 12 to 15 minutes, until firm. Trim while warm and cool completely.
  3. Beat the royal icing stiff and use it as mortar to assemble the house on a cake board. Let it set 1 hour.
  4. Decorate with candies and icing icicles, then dust the whole scene with powdered sugar snow.

9. Candy Cane Cupcakes

Tall swirls of white buttercream, a crunch of crushed candy cane and a mini cane hooked over the top of each one. They are the red-and-white exclamation points of the table.

Ingredients

  • 1 batch vanilla or chocolate cupcake batter (12 cupcakes)
  • 1 cup butter, softened
  • 4 cups powdered sugar and 2 to 3 tablespoons cream
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla and 1/4 teaspoon peppermint extract
  • 4 candy canes, crushed, for sprinkling
  • 12 mini candy canes, for toppers

Directions

  1. Bake the cupcakes in festive liners and cool completely on a rack.
  2. Beat the buttercream until pale and fluffy, flavoring it with vanilla and a whisper of peppermint.
  3. Pipe a high swirl on each cupcake with a large star tip.
  4. Sprinkle with crushed candy cane right away and hook a mini candy cane into the top of each swirl.

10. Snowman Cupcakes

A little parade of marshmallow snowmen with chocolate faces and pretzel arms — the cupcakes kids make a beeline for. Group them together so the snowmen can chat among themselves.

Ingredients

  • 12 cupcakes, any flavor, baked and cooled
  • 2 cups white buttercream
  • 12 large marshmallows, for heads
  • Melted chocolate or black icing, for faces
  • Small orange candies or icing, for carrot noses
  • Pretzel sticks, for arms
  • Desiccated coconut or white sprinkles, for snow

Directions

  1. Frost the cupcakes with smooth domes of white buttercream and shower with coconut snow.
  2. Set a marshmallow on top of each cupcake, securing it with a dot of frosting.
  3. Pipe chocolate eyes, smiles and buttons, and add a tiny orange nose.
  4. Push pretzel sticks in at an angle for arms and line the snowmen up on a platter.

11. Holiday Chocolate-Covered Strawberries

Glossy dipped berries with red and green drizzles bring fresh fruit brightness to a table full of rich bakes. Make them the morning of the party so the shells stay shiny and the berries stay perky.

Ingredients

  • 24 large strawberries, washed and dried very well
  • 8 oz dark chocolate, chopped
  • 8 oz white chocolate, chopped
  • Red and green candy melts or food coloring, for drizzle
  • Holiday sprinkles and crushed candy cane

Directions

  1. Melt the dark and white chocolates in separate bowls until smooth.
  2. Hold each berry by its leaves, dip in chocolate and let the excess drip off.
  3. Set on parchment, drizzle with the contrasting color and add sprinkles or crushed cane while wet.
  4. Chill 15 minutes to set, then arrange on a chilled platter.

12. The Big Cookie Platter

One generous board where gingerbread men, snowballs and crinkles all pile in together, gaps tucked with cranberries and rosemary. This is the grazing heart of the Christmas dessert table — the one that empties and gets refilled all evening.

Ingredients

  • 1 batch gingerbread cookie dough, for cut-out men
  • 1 batch snowball cookie dough, with chopped pecans
  • 1 batch chocolate crinkle cookie dough
  • Powdered sugar, for rolling and dusting
  • Royal icing and sprinkles, for decorating the gingerbread
  • Fresh rosemary sprigs and cranberries, for garnish

Directions

  1. Bake the gingerbread men at 350°F (175°C) for 8 to 10 minutes, cool and decorate with icing faces and buttons.
  2. Roll the snowball dough into balls, bake 12 to 14 minutes and roll twice in powdered sugar while warm.
  3. Roll the chilled crinkle dough in powdered sugar and bake 10 to 12 minutes, until crackled.
  4. Arrange the cookies by shape in rows and piles on your biggest board, then fill every gap with cranberries and rosemary sprigs.

13. Assorted Fudge Squares

Two pans, two flavors: deep chocolate fudge and white chocolate studded with cranberries, cut into neat little squares. Stacked in alternating rows, they read like a checkerboard on the table.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups semisweet chocolate chips
  • 1 can (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk, divided
  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • 2 cups white chocolate chips
  • 1/2 cup dried cranberries, chopped
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Pinch of salt and chopped pistachios (optional)

Directions

  1. Melt the dark chocolate with half the condensed milk and the butter, stir in vanilla and salt, and spread into a lined 8-inch pan.
  2. Melt the white chocolate with the remaining milk, fold in the cranberries and spread into a second lined pan.
  3. Chill both pans at least 3 hours, until firm.
  4. Cut into neat 1-inch squares with a hot knife and stack in rows, alternating the flavors.

14. Mini Pecan Pies

All the gooey, glossy charm of pecan pie, shrunk into two-bite minis with crisp shells. They travel from tray to hand without a fork in sight, which is exactly what a dessert table needs.

Ingredients

  • 24 mini pie shells, or 1 pie dough rolled and cut into muffin cups
  • 3/4 cup corn syrup or golden syrup
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar
  • 2 eggs and 2 tablespoons melted butter
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract and a pinch of salt
  • 1 1/2 cups pecan halves, roughly chopped, plus extras for the tops

Directions

  1. Fit the shells or dough circles into mini muffin tins or set the shells on a baking sheet.
  2. Whisk the syrup, sugar, eggs, butter, vanilla and salt, then stir in the pecans.
  3. Fill each shell almost to the top and crown with a perfect pecan half.
  4. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 20 to 25 minutes, until the filling is set and glossy. Cool before lifting out.

15. Mini Fruit Tarts

Crisp shells, silky cream and a jewel-box tumble of glazed fruit — these are the tarts that make everyone ask which bakery they came from. The apricot glaze makes the fruit shine under the lights.

Ingredients

  • 12 mini tart shells, baked and cooled
  • 1 1/2 cups pastry cream or lemon curd
  • 2 cups mixed fruit — berries, kiwi and mandarin segments
  • 1/4 cup apricot jam, warmed and strained, for glaze
  • Mint leaves, to finish (optional)

Directions

  1. Pipe or spoon the pastry cream or lemon curd into the shells, about three-quarters full.
  2. Arrange the fruit snugly on top, mixing colors in each tart.
  3. Brush the warm apricot glaze over the fruit for a glossy shine.
  4. Chill at least 30 minutes and serve cold, with a tiny mint leaf on each.

16. Eggnog Panna Cotta Cups

Silky little glasses of eggnog-spiced cream with a whisper of nutmeg on top. They are made a day ahead, they hold their shape on the table for hours, and they eat like a holiday custard you can carry around.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups heavy cream
  • 1 cup prepared eggnog
  • 1/3 cup sugar
  • 2 1/4 teaspoons powdered gelatin (1 packet)
  • 3 tablespoons cold milk or water, for blooming
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Freshly grated nutmeg, for dusting

Directions

  1. Sprinkle the gelatin over the cold milk and let it bloom 5 minutes.
  2. Warm the cream, eggnog and sugar until steaming and the sugar dissolves — do not boil. Off the heat, melt in the gelatin and stir in vanilla.
  3. Pour into small glasses and chill at least 4 hours, until set with a gentle wobble.
  4. Dust with fresh nutmeg right before they go on the table.

17. Hot Cocoa Station

Every great Christmas dessert table needs something warm, and this is it: a slow cooker of rich cocoa, a row of mugs and little bowls of toppings to build your own. It warms hands, fills the gaps between sweets and keeps guests lingering happily.

Ingredients

  • 6 cups whole milk and 1 cup heavy cream
  • 1/2 cup cocoa powder and 1/2 cup sugar
  • 1 cup chocolate chips
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract and a pinch of salt
  • For the station: mini marshmallows, candy canes, whipped cream and chocolate shavings in small bowls
  • Mugs, spoons and a ladle

Directions

  1. Whisk the milk, cream, cocoa, sugar and salt in a slow cooker, stir in the chocolate chips and heat on low for 1 1/2 to 2 hours, whisking now and then, until smooth and hot. Stir in vanilla.
  2. Switch the cooker to warm so the cocoa holds its temperature all evening.
  3. Set the toppings out in small bowls beside a stack of mugs, with candy canes for stirrers.
  4. Top each mug to order — or let guests build their own with marshmallows, cream and shavings.

18. Christmas Donuts

Pillowy donuts dipped in red, green and white glazes and showered with sprinkles, stacked on a stand like ornaments. They bring height, color and a little carnival fun to the spread.

Ingredients

  • 12 baked or fried donuts, plain
  • 2 cups powdered sugar and 4 to 5 tablespoons milk
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Red and green food coloring
  • Holiday sprinkles and crushed candy cane

Directions

  1. Whisk the powdered sugar, milk and vanilla into a pourable glaze and divide between three bowls. Tint one red and one green, leave one white.
  2. Dip the top of each donut in a glaze, letting the excess drip back.
  3. Add sprinkles or crushed cane while the glaze is wet.
  4. Let set 15 minutes, then stack on a cake stand or thread onto a peg board for serving.

19. Rice Krispie Christmas Trees

The classic cereal treat goes to finishing school: tinted green, shaped into little trees with pretzel trunks and sprinkle baubles. No-bake, kid-approved and sturdy enough to stand in a forest along the front of the table.

Ingredients

  • 6 cups crispy rice cereal
  • 10 oz marshmallows (about 40 large)
  • 3 tablespoons butter
  • Green food coloring
  • Holiday sprinkles and small candies, for baubles
  • Candy stars, for the tops
  • Pretzel sticks, cut short, for trunks

Directions

  1. Melt the butter and marshmallows until smooth, tint green and stir in the cereal until coated.
  2. With buttered hands, shape handfuls of the warm mixture into cone-shaped trees.
  3. Press sprinkles and small candies all over as baubles and crown each tree with a candy star.
  4. Push a pretzel stick into the base of each tree for a trunk and let them set 30 minutes before standing them up.

20. Chocolate-Dipped Pretzel Rods

Salty, crunchy, chocolatey and made for standing upright in jars like edible sparklers. They fill vertical space on the table for almost no effort, and they keep for days in a tin.

Ingredients

  • 24 pretzel rods
  • 8 oz dark chocolate, chopped
  • 8 oz white chocolate, chopped
  • Holiday sprinkles and crushed candy cane, for decorating

Directions

  1. Melt the dark and white chocolates in tall glasses or mugs for easy dipping.
  2. Dip each rod about two-thirds of the way down, twirl off the excess and drizzle with the other chocolate.
  3. Scatter with sprinkles or crushed candy cane while wet.
  4. Lay on parchment until set, then stand the rods in jars or glasses for serving.

21. Fruitcake Bites

Fruitcake, redeemed: rich, dark and fruity, cut small so nobody has to commit to a whole slice. A cap of white icing and a piece of cherry on each bite makes them look like tiny presents.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups mixed dried fruit and candied peel
  • 1/2 cup orange juice or brandy, for soaking
  • 1 cup brown sugar and 1/2 cup butter
  • 2 eggs and 1 1/4 cups flour
  • 1 teaspoon mixed spice and 1/2 cup chopped nuts
  • Marzipan or white icing, for the tops
  • Glacé cherry pieces, to decorate

Directions

  1. Soak the fruit in the juice or brandy for at least 2 hours, or overnight.
  2. Cream the butter and sugar, beat in the eggs, then fold in flour, spice, nuts and the soaked fruit. Spread in a lined square pan.
  3. Bake at 300°F (150°C) for 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 hours, until dark and firm. Cool and mature a few days if you can.
  4. Cut into small squares, top each with marzipan or white icing and a piece of cherry.

22. Stollen Slices with Snowy Sugar

Thick, buttery slices of stollen fanned on a board under a fresh snowfall of powdered sugar. Bought or baked, it brings old-world Christmas market energy — and it slices and serves in seconds.

Ingredients

  • 1 stollen loaf, bought or homemade (about 1 1/2 lb)
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter (optional, to refresh)
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar, for a heavy dusting
  • Extra dried fruit or toasted almonds, to scatter (optional)

Directions

  1. If the loaf has been stored, brush it lightly with melted butter to refresh the crust.
  2. Cut thick slices, about 3/4 inch, with a serrated knife.
  3. Fan the slices, overlapping, on a wooden board.
  4. Sieve a generous snowfall of powdered sugar over the board right before serving.

23. Cranberry Orange Bundt Cake

Your second-showstopper insurance policy: a golden bundt bright with orange zest, studded with tart cranberries and dripping with glaze. It holds the other end of the table opposite the red velvet, and it slices like a dream.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour and 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1 cup butter, softened, and 1 3/4 cups sugar
  • 4 eggs
  • Zest of 2 oranges and 1/2 cup orange juice
  • 1 cup sour cream
  • 2 cups fresh cranberries, tossed in a spoonful of flour
  • For the glaze: 1 1/2 cups powdered sugar and 3 tablespoons orange juice
  • Sugared cranberries, to crown

Directions

  1. Cream the butter, sugar and orange zest, beat in the eggs one at a time, then fold in the flour mixture alternating with sour cream and juice. Fold in the cranberries.
  2. Scrape into a well-greased bundt pan and bake at 350°F (175°C) for 50 to 60 minutes, until a tester comes out clean.
  3. Cool 15 minutes, turn out and cool completely.
  4. Pour the orange glaze over the top so it drips down the ridges, and crown with sugared cranberries.

24. Raspberry White Chocolate Mousse Cups

Pale, silky white chocolate mousse layered with sharp raspberry sauce in little glasses — the elegant cool-down after all that spice and caramel. The pink layers look especially pretty in a row.

Ingredients

  • 8 oz white chocolate, chopped
  • 2 cups heavy cream, divided
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 2 cups raspberries, fresh or frozen
  • 3 tablespoons sugar and 1 teaspoon lemon juice, for the sauce
  • Fresh raspberries and white chocolate shavings, to top

Directions

  1. Simmer the raspberries, sugar and lemon juice for 5 minutes, strain for a smooth sauce and chill.
  2. Melt the white chocolate with half the cream, cool to lukewarm, then fold in the remaining cream, whipped to soft peaks with the vanilla.
  3. Layer mousse and raspberry sauce in small glasses, finishing with mousse.
  4. Chill at least 3 hours and top with fresh raspberries and white chocolate shavings.

25. Meringue Christmas Trees

Tiny green meringue trees with sprinkle baubles and star crowns — crisp, light and almost too cute to eat. Almost. Stand them in a snowy line of powdered sugar at the very front of the table.

Ingredients

  • 4 egg whites, at room temperature
  • 1 cup superfine sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon cream of tartar
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Green gel food coloring
  • Star sprinkles and small round sprinkles, for baubles

Directions

  1. Beat the whites with the cream of tartar to soft peaks, add the sugar gradually and beat to stiff, glossy peaks. Tint green.
  2. Pipe stacked stars in three shrinking layers on parchment to form little trees.
  3. Scatter sprinkle baubles over the trees and add a star sprinkle on top of each.
  4. Bake at 200°F (95°C) for 1 1/2 to 2 hours, until dry and crisp, then cool in the turned-off oven.

Dessert Table Styling Tips

  • Build height with cake stands and stacked books under the cloth — tall in the middle, small bites at the front.
  • Stick to a 3-color palette (red, white and green here) so different desserts still match.
  • Add little tent cards naming each dessert — guests with allergies will thank you.
  • Set the table the night before with stands and platters only, and add the desserts in the last hour.

Final Thoughts

A great Christmas dessert table is really just good planning wearing a pretty dress: two cakes for drama, a crowd of little things to graze on, one warm station, and everything made ahead except the final dusting of sugar. Pick the showstoppers first, fill in with minis, and leave room on the table for the tent cards and a few sprigs of greenery. When the doorbell rings, light the candles, pour yourself a cocoa and watch everyone drift, exactly as planned, back to the sweetest room in the house.
