25+ Christmas Crinkle Cookies with Crackled Snow Tops

Oct 24, 2026

Crinkle cookies are pure Christmas magic — a soft ball of dough rolled twice in sugar, baked until the top splits into snowy cracks. These Christmas crinkle cookies take that one trick and run with it through chocolate, citrus, spice and red velvet, so your tin looks like a snowstorm and tastes like December.

Every cookie on this list starts the same way — chill the dough until firm, roll it first in granulated sugar and then in a thick snowfall of powdered sugar — yet each flavor twists the formula in a new direction. Bake one tray or work through all twenty-five, and Christmas crinkle cookies will make the whole kitchen smell like a holiday bakery.

1. Classic Chocolate Crinkle Cookies

The original Christmas crinkle cookies, and still the benchmark: deep, fudgy cocoa centers hiding under a bold black-and-white crackle. If you bake only one batch this season, make it this one.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 cup vegetable oil or melted butter
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar, for rolling
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar, for rolling

Directions

  1. Whisk the cocoa, sugar and oil until glossy, beat in the eggs and vanilla, then stir in the flour, baking powder and salt. Chill the dough for at least 2 hours.
  2. Scoop tablespoon-sized balls, roll each one first in granulated sugar, then generously in powdered sugar until thickly coated.
  3. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 10–12 minutes, until deeply crackled with soft centers. Cool on the tray for 5 minutes.

2. Red Velvet Crinkle Cookies

All the drama of red velvet cake in cookie form — a whisper of cocoa, a vivid red crumb and snowy white cracks that look made for a Christmas tin.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 3/4 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 tbsp red food coloring
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tbsp unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • 1/3 cup cream cheese chips (optional)
  • Granulated and powdered sugar, for rolling

Directions

  1. Beat the butter and both sugars until fluffy, then beat in the egg, vanilla and red coloring. Stir in the flour, cocoa, baking powder and salt, plus the chips if using, and chill for at least 2 hours.
  2. Roll tablespoon balls first in granulated sugar, then thickly in powdered sugar.
  3. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 10–12 minutes, until the red cracks show through the snow. Cool on the tray for 5 minutes.

3. Lemon Crinkle Cookies

Bright, sunny and unmistakably lemony, these pale-gold crinkles taste like a bite of winter citrus. They lighten up a tin full of chocolate and spice beautifully.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tbsp finely grated lemon zest
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 tsp baking powder
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • Granulated and powdered sugar, for rolling

Directions

  1. Beat the butter and sugar until pale, then beat in the egg, lemon juice and zest. Stir in the flour, baking powder and salt, and chill for at least 2 hours.
  2. Roll tablespoon balls first in granulated sugar, then generously in powdered sugar.
  3. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 10–12 minutes, until crackled at the edges and just set in the middle. Cool on the tray for 5 minutes.

4. Gingerbread Crinkle Cookies

All the molasses warmth of gingerbread, but soft and crackled instead of cut and iced. The spice blooms as they bake, and the dark dough makes the snowy top pop.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1/3 cup molasses
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 3/4 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp ground ginger
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 tsp ground cloves
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • Granulated and powdered sugar, for rolling

Directions

  1. Beat the butter and brown sugar, then beat in the molasses and egg. Stir in the flour, spices, baking soda and salt, and chill for at least 2 hours.
  2. Roll tablespoon balls first in granulated sugar, then thickly in powdered sugar.
  3. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 10–12 minutes, until crackled and fragrant. Cool on the tray for 5 minutes.

  • 1/4 tsp fine salt

Directions

9. Peanut Butter Crinkle Cookies

Yes, peanut butter crinkles work — and no fork marks required, because the crackle does all the decorating. Salty, nutty and soft in the middle.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 3/4 cup creamy peanut butter
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 1/4 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 tsp baking soda
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • Granulated and powdered sugar, for rolling

Directions

  1. Beat the butter, peanut butter and both sugars until fluffy, then beat in the egg and vanilla. Stir in the flour, baking soda and salt, and chill for at least 2 hours.
  2. Roll tablespoon balls first in granulated sugar, then generously in powdered sugar — skip the fork, the cracks are the pattern.
  3. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 10–12 minutes, until crackled and soft. Cool on the tray for 5 minutes.

13. Strawberry Crinkle Cookies

Soft pink crackles with a real berry sweetness — the cookies kids reach for first and adults quietly take seconds of. A cake-mix shortcut works here, too.

Ingredients

  • 1 box (about 15 oz) strawberry cake mix, or a vanilla dough plus 1/2 cup freeze-dried strawberry powder
  • 1/3 cup vegetable oil or melted butter
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • Granulated and powdered sugar, for rolling

Directions

  1. Stir the cake mix (or strawberry dough), oil, eggs and vanilla into a soft dough and chill for at least 2 hours — this dough is sticky.
  2. Roll tablespoon balls first in granulated sugar, then generously in powdered sugar.
  3. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 9–11 minutes, until pink cracks show and the centers stay soft. Cool on the tray for 5 minutes.

14. Funfetti Crinkle Cookies

15. Snickerdoodle Crinkle Cookies

A snickerdoodle that learned the crinkle trick: roll it in cinnamon sugar first, then in powdered sugar, and you get spiced cracks with a snowy finish. Tangy, thanks to the cream of tartar.

  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp cream of tartar
  • 1/2 tsp baking soda
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar mixed with 1 tsp cinnamon, for rolling
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar, for rolling

Directions

  1. Beat the butter and sugar, then beat in the egg and vanilla. Stir in the flour, cream of tartar, baking soda and salt, and chill for at least 2 hours.
  2. Roll tablespoon balls first in the cinnamon sugar, then generously in powdered sugar.
  3. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 10–12 minutes, until crackled and cinnamon-flecked. Cool on the tray for 5 minutes.

18. Brown Butter Crinkle Cookies

Browning the butter first adds a toasty, almost hazelnut depth to a simple vanilla dough. It is one extra pan and about five minutes for a noticeably fancier cookie.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, browned and cooled slightly
  • 3/4 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 3/4 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • Granulated and powdered sugar, for rolling

Directions

  1. Melt the butter over medium heat, swirling until amber and nutty-smelling, then cool 10 minutes. Beat with the brown sugar, egg and vanilla, stir in the dry ingredients, and chill for at least 2 hours.
  2. Roll tablespoon balls first in granulated sugar, then thickly in powdered sugar.
  3. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 10–12 minutes, until crackled with soft centers. Cool on the tray for 5 minutes.

19. Salted Caramel Crinkle Cookies

A soft caramel hides inside each dough ball and melts into a gooey center as they bake. A pinch of flaky salt on the snowy top makes the whole thing sing.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 cup vegetable oil or melted butter
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • 24 soft caramel candies, halved (or thick caramel sauce for drizzling)
  • Granulated and powdered sugar, for rolling
  • Flaky sea salt, for finishing

Directions

  1. Make the cocoa dough as for the classic crinkles and chill for at least 2 hours. Flatten each tablespoon of dough around a caramel half and seal it into a ball.
  2. Roll the sealed balls first in granulated sugar, then generously in powdered sugar.
  3. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 10–12 minutes, until crackled. Add a pinch of flaky salt at once, then cool on the tray for 5 minutes.

  • 1 tsp vanilla extract

  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • Granulated and powdered sugar, for rolling

25. Christmas Tree Crinkle Cookies

The grand finale is a platter, not just a cookie: bake a batch of red velvet or green-tinted crinkles and arrange them as a Christmas tree. It is the centerpiece your dessert table has been waiting for.

Ingredients

  • 1 batch red velvet crinkle dough (Item 2), or vanilla crinkle dough (Item 24) tinted green
  • Granulated and powdered sugar, for rolling
  • 1 star-shaped cookie or a dusting of gold sprinkles, for the topper
  • Extra powdered sugar, for snowing the platter

Directions

  1. Make and chill the crinkle dough for at least 2 hours, then roll tablespoon balls first in granulated sugar, then generously in powdered sugar.
  2. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 10–12 minutes, until crackled, and cool on the tray for 5 minutes before moving — the tops are delicate.
  3. On a large platter, arrange the cooled crinkles in rows that narrow as they rise — one at the top, then two, three and so on — to form a Christmas tree.
  4. Set the star cookie on top, add a short row of cookies as the trunk, and finish with a final snowfall of powdered sugar.

Perfect Crinkle Tips

  • Chill the dough at least 2 hours — warm dough spreads and loses its cracks
  • Roll first in granulated sugar, then generously in powdered sugar — the double coat makes the cracks pop
  • Don’t overbake — pull them when the edges are set but centers still soft
  • Cool on the tray 5 minutes so the crackled tops set before moving

Final Thoughts

That is the joy of Christmas crinkle cookies: one simple chill-and-roll method, dressed up twenty-five ways, and every single tray comes out of the oven looking like it has been snowed on. The crackled top is the star, but the flavors underneath — chocolate, citrus, spice, maple, mint — are what keep hands returning to the tin.

Start with the classic, then follow your cravings through the list and note your family favorites for next year. However you pack them, these Christmas crinkle cookies freeze well, gift well and disappear fast, so consider a double batch of the best-loved flavors.
