25+ Christmas Shortbread Cookies That Melt in Your Mouth

Oct 24, 2026

Few cookies are as Christmas as shortbread — just butter, sugar and flour, baked low until pale gold and impossibly tender. Master the classic once and these Christmas shortbread cookies let you spin it 25 ways, from chocolate-dipped fingers to millionaire’s bars, all with the same melt-in-your-mouth crumb.

This is a true shortbread deep-dive. The classic base never changes: good butter, sugar, all-purpose flour, a pinch of fine salt and a little vanilla, mixed gently so the dough stays tender, chilled so it holds its shape, and baked low so the edges stay pale. Learn that one dough and every recipe below is just a small, smart change to it — a handful of chips, a spoonful of zest, a dip of chocolate. Consider this your master class in Christmas shortbread cookies: one base, twenty-five trays, zero fuss.

1. Classic Butter Shortbread Fingers

The one to master first: plain, buttery and impossibly tender, cut into neat fingers that snap cleanly and then melt. Everything else on this list starts right here.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar, plus extra for sprinkling
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Directions

  1. Beat the butter and sugar just until smooth and creamy, then mix in the vanilla. Stir in the flour and salt until a soft dough forms — stop as soon as it comes together.
  2. Press the dough evenly into a parchment-lined 8-inch square pan, dock all over with a fork, and chill for 30 minutes.
  3. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 25–30 minutes, until pale gold at the edges. Score into fingers while warm, sprinkle with sugar, cool completely, then cut along the lines.

2. Scottish Petticoat Tails

The traditional Scottish round, named for the flared skirts it resembles. A little rice flour or cornstarch in place of some flour gives the wedges their famous delicate snap.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 1 1/2 cups (190 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup (60 g) rice flour or cornstarch
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Directions

  1. Make the base dough, using the flour and rice flour together. Roll into a smooth 8-inch round on parchment, about 1/2 inch thick.
  2. Flute the edges with your fingertips, dock with a fork, and score into 8 wedges without cutting all the way through. Chill for 30 minutes.
  3. Bake at 300°F (150°C) for 35–40 minutes until set and very pale. Cool, then break or cut along the scored lines.

3. Sugared Shortbread Rounds

Simple rounds with a sparkle: the dough is rolled, cut and chilled, then the warm cookies get a roll in sugar so every bite starts with a delicate crunch.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/3 cup coarse or granulated sugar, for coating

Directions

  1. Make the base dough, roll to 1/4 inch thick between parchment sheets, and chill for 20 minutes.
  2. Cut out 2-inch rounds, place on a lined tray, and chill again for 15 minutes so the edges stay sharp.
  3. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 14–16 minutes until just set. While warm, roll the edges or tops in the coating sugar and cool on a rack.

4. Chocolate Chip Shortbread

Mini chips are the whole trick here — small enough to slice cleanly through a chilled log, so every round is studded edge to edge with chocolate.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup mini chocolate chips

Directions

  1. Make the base dough and stir in the mini chips last, mixing just until they are evenly scattered.
  2. Shape into a log about 2 inches wide, wrap well, and chill for at least 1 hour, until very firm.
  3. Slice into 1/4-inch rounds and bake at 325°F (165°C) for 14–16 minutes, until the edges are barely golden.

5. Chocolate-Dipped Shortbread

Classic fingers, dressed up: one end goes into dark chocolate and the contrast — crumbly, buttery cookie against a snappy chocolate shell — is Christmas-cookie perfection.

Ingredients

  • 1 batch Classic Butter Shortbread Fingers (item 1), baked and cooled
  • 6 oz (170 g) dark chocolate, chopped
  • 1 teaspoon coconut oil (optional, for shine)
  • Festive sprinkles (optional)

Directions

  1. Melt the chocolate (with the coconut oil, if using) in short bursts in the microwave or over simmering water until smooth.
  2. Dip one end of each finger about a third of the way in, let the excess drip off, and lay on parchment. Add sprinkles while the chocolate is wet, if you like.
  3. Leave at room temperature until set, about 30 minutes, then store in a tin between sheets of parchment.

6. Lemon Shortbread

Bright and sunny against all that butter: lemon zest goes right into the dough and a tart little glaze goes on top once the cookies are cool.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 tablespoon lemon zest, plus 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Glaze: 3/4 cup powdered sugar whisked with 1–2 tablespoons lemon juice

Directions

  1. Rub the lemon zest into the sugar until fragrant, then make the base dough with the zesty sugar and the lemon juice.
  2. Roll, cut into rounds, chill for 30 minutes, and bake at 325°F (165°C) for 14–16 minutes. Cool completely.
  3. Drizzle or dip the tops with the lemon glaze and leave until set, about 20 minutes.

7. Cranberry Orange Shortbread Wedges

Ruby cranberries and orange zest make these wedges look like Christmas before you even taste them, and the tart-sweet chew plays beautifully against the butter.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1/2 cup dried cranberries, roughly chopped
  • 1 tablespoon orange zest
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Directions

  1. Make the base dough, rubbing the orange zest into the sugar first, then fold in the cranberries.
  2. Press into a parchment-lined 9-inch round pan, dock with a fork, score into 12 wedges, and chill for 30 minutes.
  3. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 25–28 minutes until pale gold. Cool in the pan, then cut along the scores.

8. Rosemary Sea Salt Shortbread

The savory-sweet grown-up of the list: a little less sugar, fragrant fresh rosemary in the dough, and a flicker of flaky salt on top of every slice.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/3 cup (65 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 2 teaspoons finely chopped fresh rosemary
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Flaky sea salt, for topping

Directions

  1. Make the base dough with the reduced sugar, stirring the rosemary in with the flour.
  2. Shape into a log, chill for 1 hour, then slice into 1/4-inch rounds and sprinkle each with a few flakes of sea salt.
  3. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 14–16 minutes until set and pale at the edges. Lovely with cheese boards and mulled drinks alike.

9. Espresso Shortbread

Coffee deepens everything it touches, and this dough is no exception — a gentle espresso warmth under the butter, with an optional dark chocolate dip for mocha energy.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 2 teaspoons instant espresso powder
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 4 oz dark chocolate, melted, for dipping (optional)

Directions

  1. Dissolve the espresso powder in the vanilla, then make the base dough with the coffee-vanilla mixture.
  2. Shape into a log, chill for 1 hour, and slice into 1/4-inch rounds.
  3. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 14–16 minutes. Cool, then dip one edge in melted dark chocolate if you like, and let set on parchment.

10. Brown Butter Shortbread

Browning the butter first is the whole recipe: the milk solids toast into something nutty and toffee-like, and a pinch of nutmeg makes the whole tray smell like Christmas.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, browned and cooled until just solid-soft
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Directions

  1. Melt the butter over medium heat, swirling, until the solids turn amber and smell nutty, 4–5 minutes. Pour into a bowl and chill until soft-solid, about 30 minutes.
  2. Make the base dough with the browned butter and nutmeg. Press into a lined pan or shape as rounds, then chill the dough well, at least 45 minutes — brown butter dough is soft.
  3. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 16–20 minutes until deeper gold than classic shortbread. Cool completely before slicing.

11. Pecan Shortbread

Toasted pecans fold right into the dough, so the cookies bake up sandy, nutty and rich — like a pecan sandie that went to finishing school.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 3/4 cup toasted pecans, finely chopped

Directions

  1. Toast the pecans at 350°F (175°C) for 6–8 minutes until fragrant; cool and chop finely.
  2. Make the base dough and fold the pecans in at the end. Shape into a log or roll and cut rounds; chill for 30 minutes.
  3. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 15–17 minutes until lightly golden at the edges.

12. Maple Shortbread

Maple sugar in the dough gives these wedges a warm, woodsy sweetness that plain sugar cannot, and a quick maple glaze doubles down on the cozy.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) maple sugar (or 1/3 cup sugar plus 3 tablespoons maple syrup and 1 extra tablespoon flour)
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Glaze: 1/2 cup powdered sugar whisked with 2 tablespoons maple syrup

Directions

  1. Make the base dough with the maple sugar. Press into a lined 9-inch round pan, score into wedges, and chill for 30 minutes.
  2. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 25–28 minutes until set and lightly golden.
  3. Cool, cut along the scores, and drizzle with the maple glaze. Leave until set before storing.

13. Ginger Shortbread

All the warmth of gingerbread with shortbread’s tender crumb: ground ginger for background heat, crystallized ginger bits for little sparks, and sugary tops.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar, plus extra for tops
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons ground ginger
  • 2 tablespoons crystallized ginger, very finely chopped
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Directions

  1. Make the base dough, whisking the ground ginger in with the flour and folding the crystallized ginger in last.
  2. Roll and cut into rounds, chill for 30 minutes, and sprinkle the tops with sugar.
  3. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 14–16 minutes until set and fragrant.

14. Lavender Shortbread

Delicate and a little bit fancy: just a teaspoon of culinary lavender, rubbed into the sugar so the floral note perfumes the dough instead of shouting over it.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon culinary lavender buds
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Directions

  1. Pulse or rub the lavender buds into the sugar until fragrant, then make the base dough — a small amount is key; lavender turns soapy if you overdo it.
  2. Roll into small rounds or shape into a log and slice. Chill for 30 minutes.
  3. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 13–15 minutes, keeping them pale. Cool on a rack.

15. Earl Grey Shortbread

Finely ground tea leaves fleck the dough and lend their bergamot perfume, so these slice-and-bake rounds taste like a cup of Earl Grey in cookie form.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • Leaves from 2 Earl Grey tea bags, finely ground
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Directions

  1. Grind the tea leaves to a fine powder with a mortar or spice grinder and whisk into the flour.
  2. Make the base dough, shape into a log, wrap, and chill for at least 1 hour.
  3. Slice into 1/4-inch rounds and bake at 325°F (165°C) for 14–16 minutes. Serve, of course, with tea.

16. Almond Shortbread

Almond extract perfumes the dough and a cap of sliced almonds, pressed on before baking, toasts into the prettiest finish on the list.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon almond extract (in place of vanilla)
  • 1/2 cup sliced almonds, for topping

Directions

  1. Make the base dough with almond extract instead of vanilla. Roll and cut into rounds; chill for 20 minutes.
  2. Press a few sliced almonds gently onto the top of each round so they stick.
  3. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 14–16 minutes, until the almonds are lightly golden.

17. Jam Thumbprint Shortbread

Little butter cups for jewel-bright jam: roll the dough into balls, press a thumbprint in the middle, and let raspberry or apricot do the decorating.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup raspberry or apricot jam
  • Powdered sugar, for dusting

Directions

  1. Make the base dough and roll into 1-inch balls. Place on a lined tray and press a deep thumbprint into each; chill for 20 minutes.
  2. Fill each dent with about 1/2 teaspoon jam.
  3. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 14–16 minutes until set and pale gold. Cool, then dust lightly with powdered sugar, avoiding the jam.

18. Millionaire’s Shortbread

The richest tray on the list: a crisp shortbread base, a thick layer of golden caramel, and a cap of chocolate, sliced into bars that live up to the name.

Ingredients

  • 1 batch shortbread base dough (item 1)
  • Caramel: 1/2 cup (113 g) butter
  • Caramel: 1/2 cup (100 g) brown sugar
  • Caramel: 3/4 cup sweetened condensed milk, plus a pinch of salt
  • Topping: 1 1/2 cups chocolate chips, melted with 1 teaspoon coconut oil

Directions

  1. Press the base dough into a parchment-lined 8-inch square pan, dock, and bake at 325°F (165°C) for 22–25 minutes until pale gold. Cool.
  2. Simmer the caramel ingredients, stirring constantly, for 5–7 minutes until thick and golden. Pour over the base and chill until firm, about 1 hour.
  3. Spread the melted chocolate over the caramel, let it just set, then slice into bars with a warm knife.

19. Shortbread Sandwich Cookies

Two thin, tender rounds with a swoop of chocolate-hazelnut spread or jam between them — small, rich, and far too easy to eat two of.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) powdered sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 3/4 cup chocolate-hazelnut spread or seedless jam, for filling
  • Powdered sugar, for dusting

Directions

  1. Make the dough with powdered sugar for an extra-tender crumb. Roll thin, about 1/8 inch, cut small rounds, and chill for 20 minutes.
  2. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 10–12 minutes until just set. Cool completely.
  3. Spread the flat side of half the cookies with filling, sandwich with the rest, and dust the tops with powdered sugar.

20. Peppermint Shortbread

Cool peppermint in the dough, a white chocolate dip, and a snow of crushed candy cane: these taste like the inside of a candy shop at Christmas.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon peppermint extract (plus the vanilla, if you like)
  • 6 oz (170 g) white chocolate, melted
  • 3 candy canes, finely crushed

Directions

  1. Make the base dough with the peppermint extract. Roll, cut into rounds or fingers, chill for 30 minutes, and bake at 325°F (165°C) for 14–16 minutes. Cool.
  2. Dip half of each cookie in melted white chocolate and lay on parchment.
  3. Sprinkle immediately with crushed candy cane and leave until set.

21. Toffee Shortbread Bars

Pan bars with toffee bits baked right in, then a layer of chocolate chips melted over the hot top and spread thin — buttery, crunchy, and dangerously snackable.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 3/4 cup toffee bits
  • 1 cup chocolate chips, for the top

Directions

  1. Make the base dough and fold in the toffee bits. Press into a parchment-lined 8-inch square pan and dock with a fork; chill for 20 minutes.
  2. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 22–25 minutes until golden at the edges.
  3. Scatter the chocolate chips over the hot bars, wait 2 minutes, then spread into a thin layer (or leave as a drizzle). Cool, then slice into bars.

22. Vanilla Bean Shortbread

For vanilla lovers: the seeds of a real bean, scraped straight into the sugar, so every pale round is flecked and fragrant in a way extract alone never manages.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar, plus extra for tops
  • Seeds of 1 vanilla bean (or 2 teaspoons vanilla bean paste)
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Directions

  1. Split the vanilla bean, scrape out the seeds, and rub them into the sugar until it smells like ice cream. Make the base dough with the vanilla sugar.
  2. Roll and cut into rounds, chill for 30 minutes, and sprinkle the tops with a little extra sugar.
  3. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 14–16 minutes, keeping them pale so the vanilla shines.

23. Christmas Tree Shortbread Cut-Outs

A sturdier version of the base — one egg yolk makes the dough roll and cut like a dream — ready for tree cutters and the simplest green-sugar decorating.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 1 egg yolk
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Green sanding sugar or simple icing, for decorating

Directions

  1. Make the base dough, beating the egg yolk in with the butter and sugar for a sturdier, roll-out dough. Chill for at least 1 hour.
  2. Roll to 1/4 inch, cut out trees, and place on a lined tray. Chill the cut-outs for 15 minutes so the branches stay crisp.
  3. Bake at 325°F (165°C) for 11–13 minutes until just set. Cool, then decorate simply with green sugar or a zigzag of icing.

24. Whipped Shortbread with a Cherry Top

The cloud of the family: cornstarch in the dough and a long, long whip turn these into feather-light rosettes that dissolve on your tongue, each crowned with a cherry.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (60 g) powdered sugar
  • 1 1/2 cups (190 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup (60 g) cornstarch
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Candied cherry halves, for topping

Directions

  1. Whip the butter and powdered sugar with an electric mixer for 8–10 minutes, until very pale and fluffy — this long whip is the whole secret.
  2. Fold in the flour, cornstarch and salt gently. Pipe rosettes onto a lined tray (or drop by spoonfuls) and press a cherry half into the center of each.
  3. Bake low at 300°F (150°C) for 14–16 minutes, until set but not browned. Cool on the tray — they are fragile while warm.

25. Pistachio Slice-and-Bake Shortbread

The prettiest slice on the tray: a log rolled in chopped pistachios so every round is framed in green, with a whisper of orange zest if you want it festive.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 3/4 cup pistachios, finely chopped, divided
  • 1 teaspoon orange zest (optional)

Directions

  1. Make the base dough (with the orange zest rubbed into the sugar, if using) and fold in half the pistachios.
  2. Shape into a log, roll the outside in the remaining pistachios, wrap, and chill for at least 2 hours, until very firm.
  3. Slice into 1/4-inch rounds and bake at 325°F (165°C) for 14–16 minutes until pale gold at the edges.

Shortbread Secrets

Great Christmas shortbread cookies are mostly about restraint — here is what matters most.

  • Use the best butter you can — it is the flavor. With only a handful of ingredients, good butter is the difference between fine and unforgettable.
  • Keep the dough cool and the oven low so the edges stay pale; chill shaped dough before baking and never rush it into a hot oven.
  • Dock the dough with a fork so it bakes flat and even, releasing steam instead of puffing or bubbling.
  • Shortbread keeps 2 weeks in a tin and the flavor deepens — bake early, tin them up, and let Christmas week be easy.

Final Thoughts

One dough, twenty-five trays, and a tin that never seems to stay full for long — that is the quiet magic of Christmas shortbread cookies. Master the classic fingers first, notice how the butter, the chill and the low oven do the work, and then let the variations carry you: lemon for the bright days, brown butter when you want cozy, millionaire’s bars when only the richest tray will do.

However you spin them, these Christmas shortbread cookies are the kind of baking that makes a house smell like Christmas and a gift box feel generous. Bake a double batch, share half, and keep a few fingers hidden at the back of the tin for yourself — you have earned them.
